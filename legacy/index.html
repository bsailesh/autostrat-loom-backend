Why now

Companies are hiring product managers for judgment. Most of the job is still admin.

The role is splitting into two tracks — strategic and executional — but budgets rarely fund both. Here's the gap Loom is built to close.

The generalist PM is stretched past what one person can hold

Growth bets, internal R&D, customer-funded commitments, sustainment fixes, and obsolescence risk all compete for the same roadmap — usually triaged by instinct, under one overloaded owner.

AI tools without an operating layer

Leadership is buying AI licenses faster than teams can adopt them. The tools exist. The workflow that turns them into daily habit — usually doesn't.

Sustainment and obsolescence get ignored

Most product tools are built for shipping net-new features. The unglamorous work of keeping fielded products alive rarely gets a system of its own.

Senior leadership wants a seat at the strategy table

Executives are more involved in setting product direction than they were two years ago. That only works if the underlying prioritization is defensible, not vibes-based.

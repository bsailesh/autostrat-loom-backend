The gap

Aerospace and defense programs run on decades, not sprints.

A fighter platform or transport aircraft can stay in service 30+ years past its last production unit. Over that lifecycle, product and program leaders are expected to track airworthiness compliance exposure (DO-178C, ARP4754A), export-control boundaries (ITAR, EAR), diminishing manufacturing sources as commercial component suppliers exit the market (DMSMS), and mission-readiness signal — often across systems that were never built to talk to each other.

The usual response is more headcount, more spreadsheets, or a generic AI assistant bolted onto existing tools. None of it holds. A spreadsheet doesn't track a DMSMS Last Time Buy window. A chatbot doesn't segregate ITAR-controlled data by default, and it wasn't built to reason in the terms your program actually runs on.

Not a software backlog tool

Software teams ship on sprints. Your programs move through stage gates — concept, feasibility, development, qualification, production, sustainment — where every decision has to hold up against engineering maturity (TRL), manufacturability (DFM/DFA), verification & validation, and regulatory compliance, not just adoption metrics. Loom's agents are built around that reality: the sustainment agent reasons in DMSMS and reliability terms, not feature requests; the synthesis agent scores imperatives against compliance and mission-readiness exposure, alongside customer demand — the same way a gate review would.
